## What this book is

Growing older does not happen in one day. It arrives in stages: first the body sends signals, then someone has to help at home, then come the conversations nobody wants to start. This book is arranged along that timeline — **five stages, 23 chapters, 6 appendices, roughly 155,000 Chinese characters**.

It is not a to-do list. Most entries you will use once or twice in a lifetime; you can start with any single entry that fits where you are now.

## How to use it

- **First time**: start with [Chapter 0 · how to use this book](https://aging.fly2ai.top/00-第0章-这份说明书怎么用/) (Chinese).
- **You or a parent has a symptom**: Appendix B is a symptom lookup (pain, leaking, forgetting, falls).
- **Something urgent and you are not sure**: Appendix D, when you must call 120 (China's emergency number).
- **Something weighing on your mind**: Appendix C is a fear lookup (fear of poverty, of burdening your children, of loneliness, of dying in pain).
- **Money and entitlements**: Appendix A, a national ledger of schemes, each line with its policy basis and the date it was checked.
- **What to read next**: Appendix F, further reading (narrative and ethics, caregiving, official sources).

## Evidence grades

Every figure carries a grade: **A** = a public source with a specific number you can look up; **B** = research support but no single number; **C** = common practice, not evidence. Where a claim could not be traced, the text says so in the open.

## Boundaries

- Compiled from **public sources**. Not medical advice, and no substitute for your doctor or your own judgement.
- The compiler is not a doctor and does not pretend to be an older person. There is **no self-testing** anywhere in this book.
- No dosages, no prescriptions, no diagnoses — only who to ask and what to ask.
- No financial relationship with anything or anyone recommended in the book.
