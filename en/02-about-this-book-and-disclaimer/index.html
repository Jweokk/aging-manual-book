    About this material, and what it does not take on¶

    This is not a commissioned book and it is not an institutional project.

    It began with something small: as I got older, I wanted to know for myself — what to watch for after a fall, how a tableful of medicines should actually be sorted, how far I can stand in for my parents when they can't get things done on a phone, and who to go to for which procedure when that day comes. I went looking, and found the material is all there, just scattered: half of it on government and hospital websites, half of it in guidelines and journals, the rest in news reports — and with a fair amount of it contradicting itself.

    With AI to help, it can now be put together quickly into the manual you are reading. It was written to the standard of "I will need this myself later": every entry has to have a source you can check; where it does not, it says "not found" — no fudging, not even for myself.

    Having looked it all up anyway, it seemed better to put it out. There are probably plenty of people who need it the way I did, and every one who can be saved the work is worth it. Hence this open-source collection of material.

    Read these few lines first¶

    This is not medical advice. I don't see patients and I don't prescribe. There is not one dose, not one prescription, not one diagnosis in this book. Wherever it differs from what your doctor says, follow the doctor in front of you who has seen you in person. In an emergency call 120 first, don't reach for a book.

    This is not legal advice. Guardianship, wills, inheritance, signatures — practice varies a lot by locality, and the regulations keep changing. This book tells you only that the thing exists, roughly which window to ask at, and which questions to ask. For how it actually works, ask a notary office, a lawyer, or the department in charge.

    This is not investment advice. I recommend no products and comment on no financial product's merits. The money-related parts of this book cover two things only: how the state's systems work, and how people cheat you.

    The policies and figures in here are dated. Every source carries the date I checked it (broadly, October 2026). Policies get adjusted, phone numbers change, guidelines are updated. Before you act, check again yourself using the leads in the book, or call 12345 / 12333 and ask. Anything marked unverified is a place where I could not reach the original document — treat that claim with care.

    Where I am not sure, I say so. There are more than two hundred such notes across the book, all listed in each chapter's closing section. These are not boilerplate disclaimers; they are places where I genuinely could not check. If you find the source, I would be glad to hear it; if you find an error, more glad still.

    Whether to act on it is your call¶

    All this book can do is lay out what was found, and where it came from. Whether to act on it, you know better than I do: your illness, your medicines, your family, how much money and time you have.

    For anything involving medication, exercise, diet or care arrangements, ask a doctor first. For anything involving signatures, property or guardianship, ask a lawyer or a notary first. If you do something because this book said so, the outcome is yours to carry.

    Corrections¶

    This book will change. Wrongly checked, wrongly written, policies replaced — all of it gets changed, and each change is recorded in the version notes.

    Where to report a problem:

    • Open an Issue in the GitHub repository: https://github.com/Jweokk/aging-manual-book/issues
    • Email: weokk2025@gmail.com

    If you know the phone number, subsidy or office window in your city differs from what is written here, please send that too.

    One last line¶

    This is something I looked up and sorted out for my own use with the help of AI, put out in passing for others to consult. It does not replace your judgement, and it does not tell you how to live.

    Take what is useful, turn past what is not.
