    The Aging Manual¶

    What you, or your parents, do next.

    A practical manual for ordinary people in China: how the body ages, what to check, what to ask for, and who to ask. It does not teach you how to grow old — it asks only that you can find the right page when something goes wrong.

    Read in Chinese (中文版) GitHub repository

    What this is¶

    In China there is no shortage of books about growing old. What is missing is the one you can follow. Translations of good foreign books — Being Mortal, The Book About Getting Older — talk about attitude and conversation, but their institutions, money and paperwork are foreign. Official materials are authoritative and free, but they sit scattered across dozens of documents from a dozen departments, one document to one subject, and none of them is written in language a reader can use.

    This manual tries to close that gap: Chinese context, the whole arc of aging, an evidence grade and an official entry point on every entry, open source and correctable. It is arranged by the four stages of aging — build your reserves; read the warning signs; arrange the home, the money and the people; then the last stretch.

    How to read it¶

    Every entry follows the same shape: what it costs, in short, what you can do, where this comes from, who this is for, what you get back, and — for the Chinese system — how this works in China.

    Every figure carries a grade: A means a specific, publicly checkable source; B means research support without an exact figure; C means common practice, not evidence. Where a source is second-hand it is marked second-hand; where we could not check it at all, unverified. There is no dose, no prescription and no diagnosis anywhere in this book — only who to ask and what to ask.

    English edition: in progress¶

    The Chinese edition is the original and is complete. The English edition is being translated and published in batches, so some sections are not here yet.

    Batch Contents Status
    Front matter Chapter 0 · Foreword · About this book Available
    0 What this is · how to read it Available
    1 part 1 and part 2 (Chapters 1–8) Available
    2 parts 3 and 4 (Chapters 9–20) Available
    3 part 5, afterword, appendices (Chapters 21–23, A–F) Planned

    Chapters that are not yet translated are simply absent from the navigation on the left. Each chapter is translated against the Chinese source and checked section by section — headings, figures, links and the evidence labels all have to match the original — so this is not an unreviewed machine translation of the whole book. Where the subject is China's own systems — long-term care insurance, medical insurance, guardianship, funeral procedures — the English text explains the mainland Chinese system and notes how it differs from what readers elsewhere may know (LTC insurance, Medicare, power of attorney). It does not argue that one system is better.

    The full Chinese edition, with all 23 chapters and 6 appendices, is at aging.fly2ai.top.
